# 03 — Market and ICP

## The qualifying filter

**Licensure, not headcount.** A licensed operator carries a named compliance function regardless of size, because the regulator will not issue the license otherwise. This means the approval capability Heed's sale depends on exists at company sizes where you would normally expect to find nobody able to say yes to a third-party script.

**No physical activation channel.** Betting Hero requires a retail venue in a legal state. Operators without one — federal exchanges, online-only books, couriers, on-ramps, mobile-only markets — have the identical problem and no concierge alternative.

**Funnel age.** A five-year-old flow has already had its obvious drop-off points found and fixed. A 2026 flow has not, and the operator knows it.

## Tier 1 — Prediction exchanges

Federally regulated, so compliance is structural. Exchange-style onboarding genuinely confuses a bettor arriving from a sportsbook, who meets an order book and a contract price in cents rather than a wager. That is comprehension friction, which is the `confusion` and `trust_gap` classes the net already handles.

**Novig** — peer-to-peer sports exchange, reportedly near $500M valuation with over $5B cumulative volume. Sharpest single fit. *Status: contact Jacob Fortinsky declined by DM in July 2026 after five unanswered follow-ups. He never saw the product; the decline was of the approach, not the fit. Do not re-approach another contact there before Q4 2026, and only with a public artifact or a signed pilot in hand.*

**Pascal** — $9M Series A mid-July 2026, NYC, aimed at serious traders and institutions. Deliberately complex product, funnel measured in weeks. Highest-priority cold approach: they have not yet formed an opinion about Heed or accumulated their own conversion data.

**ProphetX** — CFTC-regulated, active conversation.

**Railbird**, **Sporttrade** — same structural position.

**Predict Street** — Gibraltar-licensed, first prediction market licensed in Europe, FIFA's official prediction partner for the 2026 World Cup. The UK/EU compliance-bar prospect where the regulatory work becomes an asset rather than overhead.

*Caveat to state honestly in any pitch:* the category is not settled. Arizona's AG filed criminal charges against Kalshi, it is unavailable in several states, and the CFTC swaps classification faces active challenge. The difference from sweepstakes is directional — these operators are fighting to be brought further inside federal regulation, and attracting institutional capital while they do it.

## Tier 2 — Licensed DFS and pick'em

Multi-state licensed, compliance lead as a licensing condition, none large enough to have built this internally, all squeezed simultaneously by state DFS crackdowns and by prediction markets pulling users sideways. This is where the Betting Hero evidence lands hardest — they watch the major books buy human activation and know they cannot afford it.

**PrizePicks** (CEO-level conversation active), **Dabble**, **Sleeper**, **Betr**, **Boom Fantasy**, **Chalkboard**, **ParlayPlay**, **OwnersBox**.

**Underdog** — worth a swing despite size. Laid off dozens in March 2026 during the business transition, meaning fewer internal engineers, and now runs a brand-new national exchange funnel alongside DFS.

## Tier 3 — Regulated on-ramps and stablecoin rails

Money-transmitter obligations force the same compliance headcount. The fee-disclosure hesitation pattern is the exact signal chain already demonstrated end-to-end on device.

**Banxa** (Head of Partner Integrations, active), **Transak**, **Ramp Network**, **MoonPay**. Plus regulated remittance — **MoneyGram** launched MGUSD in June 2026 for treasury and cross-border settlement, same KYC-to-first-send funnel at larger volume.

The January 2027 GENIUS deadline is the reason to talk now, and it expires.

## Tier 4 — Brazil

The densest ICP match anywhere in this document, held at Tier 4 on reachability rather than fit. 87 operators with active licenses as of Q3 2026 plus 14 pending, every one carrying a compliance function by law. Mandatory CPF validation and biometric Face Match at onboarding under SPA Ordinance 722/2024, with the industry itself acknowledging the resulting registration drop-off.

Barriers are Portuguese-language outbound, a different conference circuit, timezone offset, and no existing relationships. Enforcement is live — SECAP removed 26 operators in H1 2026 for COAF and SIGAP failures — so filter against current license status before any outreach.

The pilot proposition here is narrower and more provable than anything above it, because the friction point is known in advance and identical across operators.

## Tier 5 — Horse racing ADW and lottery couriers

Lowest competitive noise in the entire market. Nobody is pitching these operators behavioral inference.

ADW platforms run under the Interstate Horseracing Act plus state licensing, with register-to-fund-to-wager funnels and sustained pressure to convert a younger demographic that finds exotic wager types opaque. **AmWager**, **Xpressbet**, **NYRA Bets**, **TwinSpires**.

Lottery couriers run the cleanest version of the funnel under tightening state scrutiny. **Jackpot.com** (Strategy & Data contact active), **Lotto.com**, **TheLotter**.

## Channel plays — not direct accounts

Longer cycle, many-operator distribution from one integration. Parallel motion, not a substitute.

**Platform providers** — SoftSwiss, EveryMatrix, Bragg, GAN, and the Brazilian suppliers serving those 87 operators. **KYC and geolocation vendors** — GeoComply, Jumio, Persona, Sumsub, and the biometric vendors serving Face Match. These sit physically inside the friction Heed instruments and their sales conversation already covers onboarding drop-off. The operator-versus-supplier test currently gating outbound is the wrong question for them: they are distribution, not end customers. **Ontario** — iGO's registered operators are English-language, close, and governed at a UKGC/MGA-adjacent bar.

## Explicitly disqualified — sweepstakes

Superficially the strongest ICP in the category. Do not pursue it. At least 17 states have banned or restricted sweepstakes gaming, six operators have permanently shut down since October 2025, and Illinois issued 65 cease-and-desist letters in May 2026 alone naming VGW, Fliff, Stake.us, Pulsz, Modo, and High 5. Pragmatic Play, Evolution, and Hacksaw pulled their games, and the LA City Attorney's action against Stake.us named those suppliers as co-defendants.

Suppliers are being named. One sweepstakes logo on a customer page compromises the compliance-native positioning with every licensed prospect in Tiers 1 through 4.

## Deliberately out of scope for now

Retail brokerages, neobanks, and consumer lending share the funnel shape and carry FINRA or CFPB compliance headcount. Adding a fourth regulated vertical before a signed pilot dilutes the story. Revisit after the first case study, not before.

## Sequencing

Tiers 1 and 2 first, on relationship warmth and timezone. Tier 3 before the January 2027 deadline loses force. Tier 4 needs a language and license-status filter built first. Tier 5 is the low-noise reserve. Channel plays run parallel on a longer clock.

## Outbound learnings

Every ask should be smaller than a meeting. A question answerable in one line from a phone — "does this step show up in your numbers, or is the bleed elsewhere?" — is what unlocks a call. Five consecutive thirty-minute asks raise the price each attempt and end in a decline.

Never assert where a prospect's drop-off is. Frame findings as where Heed would expect to create value and let the prospect define the problem. Asserting drop-off puts them in the position of defending their funnel.

Opening messages that mention a university and a vague "activation and retention" thesis get read as job-seeking. Lead with the specific observation, not the affiliation.
